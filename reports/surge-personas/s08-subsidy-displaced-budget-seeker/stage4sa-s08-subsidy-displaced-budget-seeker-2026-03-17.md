## Section 1: Expanded Demand Snapshot

### Demand Trigger

The Affordable Connectivity Program (ACP) exhausted its $14.2 billion in federal funding and ended on June 1, 2024, eliminating a $30/month broadband subsidy for 23 million enrolled households (FCC, 2024). No federal replacement has been enacted as of March 2026. The causal chain: funding lapse caused immediate bill shock, 13% of ACP recipients canceled internet service outright, another 12% planned to cancel within three months (NDIA, 2025), and an estimated 5 million households lost internet access entirely (Human-I-T, 2026). These displaced households shifted to mobile-first internet access, driving sustained search demand for "cheap phone plan," "free government phone 2026," and "affordable wireless no contract." The Lifeline program continues at $9.25/month but does not close the $30/month gap. This is a policy-created demand vacuum with no resolution timeline.

### Demand Volume

**Refined estimate: 8-15M individuals actively seeking affordable mobile connectivity.**

- 23M households enrolled in ACP at peak (FCC, 2024)
- 5M households lost internet access entirely (Human-I-T, 2026)
- 43% of all low-income households (~19M households) are "subscription vulnerable" -- at or near the poverty line with disconnection risk or affordability concerns (Benton Institute, 2025)
- 53% of former ACP participants report difficulty paying current internet bills and plan to downgrade (AARP/PRI, 2025)
- The actively-searching subset (not yet settled on an alternative, actively comparing plans) is conservatively 8-15M individuals, consistent with Stage 2S estimate

### Decay Trajectory Model

**Classification: Plateau-then-fade**

| Timeframe | Projected Demand Level | Rationale |
|-----------|----------------------|-----------|
| Now (Week 0) | 100% (baseline) | Steady-state demand from displaced households; no new policy action |
| +2 weeks | 98% | No change expected; no legislative events scheduled |
| +4 weeks | 95% | Minor natural attrition as some households find alternatives through ISP discount programs (Comcast Internet Essentials, AT&T Access) |
| +8 weeks | 85-90% | Gradual absorption into existing budget carriers (Mint Mobile, Boost, TracFone). Tax refund season (Feb-April) provides temporary budget relief, slightly reducing urgency |
| +12 weeks | 75-85% | Plateau holds unless federal legislation passes. If a new subsidy program is enacted, demand could cliff-drop to 30-40% within 2-3 weeks of announcement |

**Comparable precedent calibration:** The ACP itself was preceded by the Emergency Broadband Benefit (EBB, 2021-2022), which converted into the ACP. When EBB transitioned to ACP, demand for budget alternatives did not cliff-drop -- it gradually redirected over 8-12 weeks as households re-enrolled. This supports a plateau-then-fade model rather than a cliff-drop.

### Seasonal / Cyclical Pattern

**Cyclical (linked to policy events).** Demand is not seasonal in the traditional sense but is linked to federal budget cycles and legislative activity. Key dates:

- Congressional appropriations cycles (annual) could trigger new subsidy legislation
- FCC regulatory actions could modify Lifeline benefit amounts
- State-level programs (California, New York, others) create localized demand spikes when announced
- Back-to-school season (Aug-Sept) creates secondary urgency as families need connectivity for schoolwork

**Next expected event:** Any Congressional action on broadband affordability legislation. No specific bill is scheduled as of March 2026, but election-year dynamics (2026 midterms in November) may generate legislative proposals in Q2-Q3 2026.

### Comparable Precedent Outcomes

**Precedent 1: EBB-to-ACP transition (Dec 2021 - Feb 2022).** When EBB converted to ACP, ~10M households needed to re-enroll or find alternatives. ISPs that proactively communicated the transition and simplified re-enrollment captured the largest share of retained subscribers. Late movers (carriers that did not communicate the change) lost 15-25% of EBB subscribers permanently. Lesson: proactive outreach to at-risk subscribers during the transition window captures disproportionate share.

**Precedent 2: TracFone Lifeline subscriber growth post-Sprint merger (2020-2021).** When Sprint merged into T-Mobile and discontinued some Lifeline-eligible plans, TracFone's Lifeline brands (SafeLink, Total Wireless) absorbed displaced subscribers within 4-8 weeks. TracFone's advantage was existing Lifeline certification and distribution through Walmart/Dollar General. Late-entering MVNOs without retail distribution captured minimal share. Lesson: channel presence at the point of need (retail, community organizations) matters more than brand in this segment.

### Interview Q&As (as the Subsidy-Displaced Budget Connectivity Seeker)

**Q1 (Price anchor): What are you currently paying for phone service, and what would you expect to pay for a plan that keeps you connected after losing ACP?**

> Right now I'm on SafeLink's Lifeline plan, so basically free, but I only get 4.5 gigs and it runs out by the twentieth. I was paying nothing with ACP before. Honestly, if I have to pay, fifteen dollars is my absolute max. Ten would be better. Anything over twenty and I'm choosing between this and groceries.

**Q2 (Purchase trigger): What would make you switch to a new plan right now versus waiting?**

> If someone told me I could get unlimited data for fifteen bucks, no contract, I'd sign up today. I can't wait around -- my kid needs internet for school and I've been sitting in the McDonald's parking lot using their Wi-Fi. The thing that would make me jump is knowing there's no hidden fees. Last time I tried Boost they hit me with activation charges nobody mentioned.

**Q3 (Channel preference): Where would you go to find and buy a new phone plan?**

> I'd Google "cheap phone plan no contract" on my phone, then check WhistleOut or Tom's Guide to compare. But honestly, if my case worker at the community center told me about something, I'd trust that more than any ad. I need to be able to sign up on my phone in five minutes -- no credit check, no store visit required.

### Interview Q&As (as the Subsidy-Displaced Budget Connectivity Seeker)

**Q1 (Current solution): What are you using right now to stay connected, and how well is it working?**

> I've got SafeLink through Lifeline -- it's free but I only get 4.5 gigs of data. That's nothing when your phone is your only internet. I burn through it in two weeks just doing job applications and my daughter's homework. Then I'm stuck on 2G until the month resets. It's not really working.

**Q2 (Switching barrier): What would stop you from trying a new carrier? What would have to be true for you to switch today?**

> I'm scared I'll lose my Lifeline benefit if I switch to the wrong carrier. The government stuff is confusing -- I don't want to fill out paperwork again and find out I'm not eligible anymore. If the new carrier accepted my Lifeline credit automatically and I didn't have to re-verify, I'd switch in a heartbeat.

**Q3 (Feature priority): If a new plan could only guarantee one thing, what would it be?**

> Unlimited data. Not "unlimited" with an asterisk where they throttle you at five gigs. Real unlimited. My phone is my internet, my TV, my kid's school computer. I need it to actually work all month, not just the first two weeks. Everything else -- hotspot, whatever -- doesn't matter if the data runs out.
