## Section 1: Expanded Demand Snapshot

### Demand Trigger

Cursor crossed $2B ARR in early March 2026, doubling revenue in three months (TechCrunch, 2026-03-02). Simultaneously, Andrej Karpathy declared "agentic engineering" as the successor paradigm to vibe coding on March 8, triggering a wave of developer discourse across X, Hacker News, and dev blogs (The New Stack, 2026-03-08). These two events -- one commercial, one cultural -- converged to create an acute identity moment for developers who now self-identify as "agentic engineers." The causal chain: Cursor revenue validation proves market size, Karpathy naming crystallizes the tribe, developer media amplifies the signal across all channels simultaneously.

### Demand Volume

- **Cursor paid subscribers:** ~500,000+ individual and team seats, based on $2B ARR with blended ARPU of ~$30-40/month (Bloomberg, 2026-03-02)
- **GitHub Copilot paid users:** 15M+ as of late 2025, with continued growth into 2026 (GitHub, 2025)
- **Total addressable for "committed agentic coding tool buyer":** ~1.5-2M U.S. developers actively paying $10-200/month for AI coding tools (estimated from Cursor, Copilot, Windsurf, Claude Code subscriber bases combined)
- **Refinement from Stage 2S:** The narrower target -- developers paying $20+/month on agentic-specific tools (Cursor Pro+, Claude Code Max, Windsurf Pro) -- is approximately 600,000-800,000 in the U.S.

### Decay Trajectory Model

| Timeframe | Demand Level | Rationale |
|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| **Week 0-2 (now)** | 100% (peak) | Karpathy declaration + Cursor $2B milestone still in acute news cycle |
| **Week 4** | 85% | News cycle fades but tool adoption is sticky; developer identity solidifying |
| **Week 8** | 70% | Tribal identity holds but "hot moment" energy dissipates; becomes normalized |
| **Week 12** | 60% | Baseline demand plateau; agentic coding is a durable practice, not a fad |

**Classification: Plateau-then-fade.** Unlike pure news-cycle surges, this one is anchored in real product adoption ($2B ARR is not hype). The acute "tribe formation" window -- where developers are actively seeking identity signals and community affiliation -- will fade over 6-10 weeks. But underlying demand for developer-identity products persists because tool adoption is sticky and recurring. The surge component (tribal identity energy) fades; the structural component (high-income developers paying for tools monthly) plateaus.

### Seasonal/Cyclical Pattern

**Cyclical.** Developer tool surges recur with major product launches, funding rounds, and paradigm declarations. Comparable events: GitHub Copilot launch (June 2022), ChatGPT Code Interpreter launch (July 2023), Devin announcement (March 2024), Cursor Series B coverage (August 2025). Next expected occurrence: likely tied to the next major agentic tool launch or developer conference cycle (Google I/O May 2026, GitHub Universe late 2026).

### Comparable Precedent Outcomes

1. **GitHub Copilot launch (2022):** Developer identity surge lasted ~8 weeks at peak intensity. Companies that moved fast on "AI-native developer" positioning (Replit, Vercel) captured long-term brand equity. Late movers found the "AI developer" identity already claimed. Relevance: first-mover in developer-identity wireless has a comparable window.

2. **Devin AI announcement (March 2024):** Intense 3-week news cycle, then rapid cliff-drop as the product underdelivered. However, it triggered lasting developer discourse about AI replacing vs. augmenting engineers. Relevance: the Cursor/Karpathy surge is more durable because it is backed by real revenue ($2B ARR), not a demo.

## Section 2: Core Factor Analysis

### Factor 2: Spending and Financial Habits

**Quantitative data:**

- **Current AI coding tool spend:** $20-200/month per developer. Cursor Pro at $20/month is the mass-market anchor; Pro+ at $60 and Ultra at $200 serve power users. GitHub Copilot Pro at $10/month, Copilot Pro+ at $39/month. Claude Code at $17-100+/month. (Cursor Pricing, 2026; GitHub, 2026; Anthropic, 2026)
- **Average developer salary:** $107,500-148,000/year for mid-to-senior level (BLS, 2025; Glassdoor, 2026). Monthly take-home after taxes: ~$6,000-8,500. High disposable income segment.
- **Wireless spending anchor:** Average U.S. single-line wireless bill is $141/month (JD Power, 2025). However, tech-savvy users skew toward MVNOs and budget carriers: Visible at $25/month, US Mobile at $25-44/month, Mint at $15-30/month. Developer wireless spending likely $25-50/month.
- **Price sensitivity signals:** Developers are price-sensitive on commodity services (wireless, cloud hosting) but willingness-to-pay is high for tools that signal professional identity or provide measurable productivity gains. The $20/month Cursor Pro anchor is the key reference price.
- **Payment preferences:** Credit card auto-billing, monthly subscriptions, no contracts. Strong preference for self-serve checkout. GitHub Marketplace and direct web purchase are dominant channels.

#### Factor 2 Q&As

**Q1 (Price anchor): What are you currently paying for wireless, and what would you expect to pay for a plan built for developers?**

> I'm on Mint, paying like $30 a month. Honestly I don't think about my phone plan much. If someone made a developer-specific plan I'd expect $25-40 range -- I'm not paying T-Mobile prices for a niche brand. The value would need to come from perks, not just data.

**Q2 (Purchase trigger): What would make you buy a developer wireless plan immediately versus ignoring it?**

> If it bundled something I already pay for -- like Cursor credits or GitHub Pro -- I'd switch today. A straight wireless plan with a dev logo slapped on it? I'd scroll past. Show me the bundle math works out cheaper than what I'm paying separately and I'll sign up tonight.

**Q3 (Channel preference): Where would you find and buy something like this? What does checkout need to look like?**

> Hacker News front page or a tweet from someone I follow -- that's how I find anything. Checkout better be a single page, credit card, no phone call, no store visit. If I have to talk to a human or wait for a SIM to ship, I'm out. eSIM activation in under two minutes or forget it.

### Factor 5: Product Landscape and Switching Behavior

**Quantitative data:**

- **Current wireless solutions:** Developers over-index on MVNOs and value carriers. Estimated split among this segment: T-Mobile/Verizon/AT&T postpaid (~40%), Mint Mobile (~15%), Visible (~12%), US Mobile (~8%), Google Fi (~10%), other MVNOs (~15%). (Estimated from MVNO market data, WhistleOut, 2026)
- **Switching barriers:** Low. MVNOs are month-to-month, no contracts. eSIM makes activation trivial. Number porting is standard. The real barrier is inertia -- wireless is a "set and forget" purchase. Most developers have not thought about their phone plan in 12+ months.
- **Switching triggers:** Bundle savings (wireless + dev tools), community membership signal, latency/reliability for tethering and remote work, frustration with carrier upselling.
- **Competitive offers in market:** No MVNO currently targets the developer community with an identity-native plan. US Mobile has the closest tech-forward positioning but does not market to developers specifically. Visible by Verizon has a tech-brand aesthetic but no developer-specific features or community. The lane is open.

#### Factor 5 Q&As

**Q1 (Current solution): What are you using for wireless right now, and how well is it working?**

> Google Fi, been on it for three years. It works fine -- I literally never think about it. Coverage is decent, price is whatever. There's nothing broken about it, but there's nothing that makes me feel like it was built for someone like me either. It's just... a phone plan.

**Q2 (Switching barrier): What would stop you from trying a new wireless plan? What would have to be true for you to switch today?**

> Honestly, laziness. Porting my number feels annoying even though I know it takes ten minutes. I'd need a reason beyond "it's for developers" -- like actual savings when I factor in a tool bundle, or a community Slack that's actually good. Make the switch feel like an upgrade, not a lateral move.

**Q3 (Feature priority): If a developer wireless plan could only do one thing, what must it have?**

> Reliable hotspot with no throttling. I work from coffee shops, airports, coworking spaces. If I'm tethering my laptop to ship code and the connection drops or gets throttled to 600kbps after 10 gigs, that's a dealbreaker. Give me 50 gigs of full-speed hotspot minimum.
