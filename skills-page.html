Last March, someone called claiming to be from the IRS. The caller ID said "US Government." The man on the phone knew her address, knew she'd filed her taxes in February, and told her she owed $3,000 in back taxes or would face arrest within 48 hours. Margaret wired the money that afternoon.

Her daughter found out three days later. T-Mobile Scam Shield didn't flag it because the caller ID looked legitimate. The scam wasn't a robocall. It was a real person, running a conversation designed to create panic.

What She's Tried

Sarah bought Margaret a Medical Guardian pendant after the incident. Margaret wore it for a week. "It made me feel old," she told Sarah. "Like I'm waiting to fall." It went in a drawer.

Sarah suggested Life360 on Margaret's phone. Margaret said no. "I'm not being tracked like a teenager."

Sarah looked into ElliQ, the companion robot for seniors. $250 plus $60 a month. Margaret's reaction: "I don't need a robot. I need people to stop calling me pretending to be the government."

Nothing Sarah found addressed the actual problem: Margaret's phone rings, she answers, and nobody is watching what happens on the call.

What Changes with Gather

Gather sits inside Margaret's phone plan. Nothing to wear, install, or accept. The AI listens to every call in real time. Not recording. Listening for patterns.

The IRS call would have triggered a warning 90 seconds in. The AI detects urgency cues ("immediate action required"), financial pressure ("wire transfer"), and isolation tactics ("don't tell anyone about this"). It whispers into Margaret's ear: "This sounds like a scam. Hang up." At the same time, Sarah gets an alert with a transcript.

If Margaret dials 911 at 3am after a fall, Sarah knows at 3:01am. Not the next afternoon.

Spam calls from spoofed numbers never ring. Calls from Margaret's cardiologist always ring through. The AI knows the difference because it has the full history of every call on the line.

Margaret doesn't have to change anything about how she uses her phone. She just answers it, the way she always has. The difference is that someone is watching now.
